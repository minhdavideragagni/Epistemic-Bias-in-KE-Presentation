Bueter 2022
Bueter separates a wide use—any tendency that impacts scientific reasoning—from a narrow use—systematic deviation from truth. Neither alone solves the demarcation problem between biased and legitimately value-laden inquiry. [Sources] Bueter 2022, abstract and sections 2–3. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2021.12.002

Bueter 2022
Bueter proposes replacing an ontological understanding of bias with an epistemic one. The key question becomes whether there are good reasons to suspect that research could have been systematically better; what counts as “better” is assessed epistemically and contextually. [Sources] Bueter 2022. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2021.12.002

Adapted from Bueter 2022 → epistemic bias in KE
This is the entry point to the PhD proper. My adapted conception: a process or artefact may be qualified as epistemically biased when, in a given assessment context, there are well-grounded reasons to suspect that the way knowledge was selected, produced, transformed, represented, interpreted or evaluated could have been systematically better with respect to a relevant epistemic criterion. The following slides will unpack phenomena, indicators, evidence, criteria, context and qualification. [Source: PhD Research Proposal, 2026] Research Problem and Conceptual Framework.
